Mistake number three is one of the most overlooked factors in metabolic health: never getting morning sunlight.
Your body operates on a master clock known as your circadian rhythm. This biological clock governs when you release cortisol in the morning, when you produce melatonin at night, and—crucially—how sensitive your cells are to insulin throughout the day.
Insulin is your primary fat-storage hormone. When your circadian rhythm is out of alignment, your body's ability to handle blood sugar drops significantly. Studies link bright light early in the day to better blood sugar control and a lower body weight.
When you stay indoors under dim artificial lighting or fluorescent bulbs, your brain never receives the biological signal that the day has officially begun. Getting 10 to 30 minutes of natural outdoor light into your eyes within the first hour of waking acts as a master reset for your blood sugar regulation and your metabolism.
